# SKILL: Event Finder

**From:** Networking Worksheet (Finding Local Events, Conferences)

## When to load this skill

Load this skill when the reader is ready to show up somewhere. The book favors **local groups with
recurring events.** Connections come from casual, repeated interactions; you start to see the same
people the more you go. Recurring meetups are also low commitment: go for the talk, stay fifteen
minutes for pizza, leave when you want, and try again next month.

## Required inputs

1. **City or metro area,** and how far the reader will travel.
2. **Role and the technologies or topics** they are interested in. They do not need experience with a
   meetup's technology, as long as the people attending are the ones they want to meet.
3. **What kind of room works for them:** size, time of day, format, and whether identity-based events
   such as Out in Tech or Black in Tech would feel more comfortable.

## What this skill produces

**Recurring local groups:** name, focus, how often it meets, typical size, cost, link, and its most
recent and upcoming events with dates.

**Event aggregators:** newsletters, calendars, and mailing lists that announce tech events locally,
with what each covers, how often it sends, cost, and the sign-up link. Only sources that have
published in the last two months.

**Conferences** in the next twelve months, if the reader wants them: date, location, ticket cost,
whether registration is open, calls for speakers or volunteers, and scholarships, diversity grants,
or discounted tickets with deadlines.

**One commitment:** a single event to attend in the next month, and how long the reader plans to stay.

## The rule

**Real groups only, nothing created.** Listings go stale and models invent plausible meetups. Every
item has a link the reader can open. Anything the skill could not confirm as still active is flagged,
not listed as fact.

## Prompts the reader can run directly

The worksheet carries these. Run them in a tool that can search the web.

**Local groups:**

> Search the web for tech groups and communities that meet regularly in [CITY OR METRO AREA]. Check
> Meetup, Luma, Eventbrite organizer pages, local Slack and Discord communities, coworking and
> university calendars, and the local chapters of national groups. I am a [ROLE] interested in
> [TECHNOLOGIES OR TOPICS]. Return a table of real groups, nothing created, with the group name, what
> they focus on, how often they meet, typical size, cost, and the link. For each group, also list its
> most recent events and anything already on its upcoming calendar, with the date and the link.

**Event aggregators:**

> Search the web for newsletters, calendars, and mailing lists that announce tech events in [CITY OR
> METRO AREA]. Include city-wide tech newsletters, coworking space lists, Meetup and Luma group
> digests, and local community sites. Return a table of real, currently active sources, nothing
> created, with the name, what it covers, how often it sends, cost, and the sign-up link. Only include
> sources that have published an issue in the last two months.

**Conferences:**

> I am a [ROLE] working on [TECHNOLOGIES OR TOPICS], based in [CITY OR METRO AREA], and I can travel
> [HOW FAR, HOW OFTEN]. Search the web for real conferences in the next twelve months, nothing
> created, and return a table with the conference name, date, location, ticket cost, and the link.
> For each one, note whether registration is open, whether there is a call for speakers or
> volunteers, and whether they offer scholarships, diversity grants, or discounted tickets, with the
> deadline for each.

## What to tell the reader

- **Open every link** before you plan around it.
- **Sign up for the aggregators and follow the groups.** That is how you hear about events early.
- **Expect about a third of RSVPs to show up.**
- **Start conference planning early,** and ask your manager about an education budget. Speaking and
  volunteering are other ways in.

## What this skill does not do

It does not register you, buy tickets, or contact organizers. It finds real options and gets you to
commit to one.
