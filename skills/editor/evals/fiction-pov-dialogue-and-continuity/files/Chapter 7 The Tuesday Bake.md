# 7 The Tuesday Bake

The oven died at ten past four on Tuesday morning. Nadia heard the fan wind down before she saw the temperature drop, and she felt her stomach go cold.

She pushed her bandana back off her forehead and looked at the dial. Two hundred and ten degrees and falling. Forty loaves of rye sat proofing on the rack, and Mrs. Feld would be at the door at seven.

"Marco," she called. "The deck oven."

Marco came through from the walk-in, wiping his hands on his apron. He nodded. He looked at the dial and nodded again. He was thinking about the letter in his coat, and about how much easier it would be to leave if the oven had given out a month ago. "Pilot's out," he said. "Or the igniter."

"Can you fix it?" Nadia asked worriedly.

He nodded. "Maybe. Get me the flashlight." "Which one?" "The big one."

She noticed her hands were shaking as she pulled the flashlight from the drawer. She handed it to him, and he knelt by the oven door, and she watched him run his thumb along the gas line the way her father used to.

"You've done this before," she said.

"Twice," he hissed. "Your dad never wanted to pay for the service contract."

Nadia nodded. She is not going to cry in front of him, she thinks. Not over an oven. She wiped her brown eyes with the back of her wrist and turned to the rack of rye.

"Marc," she said quietly, "if we can't bake, I'm opening anyway. I'll sell yesterday's bread at half."

He nodded without looking up.
