## Ranking Your Products

With a cost sheet for every item, you can rank the menu. List every product with its contribution margin and its weekly units sold, then multiply the two. The result is what each product contributes to rent in a normal week. Figure 4.1 shows a ranking for a small bakery with eleven products.

![](fig-4-1.png)

*Figure 4.1: Weekly contribution by product*

The ranking usually surprises owners. When Celine Leblanc ranked her menu at her shop in Asheville, her lemon tart, which she had considered a vanity item, turned out to be her third-highest earner because it sold out by ten every morning.

About a third of new bakeries close in their first year, and the most common reason owners give is a menu too large to bake well.¹ Every bakery that offers more than twenty products fails.

## Retiring a Product

Every product on the list below the line should go, unless it brings in customers who buy something else. Before cutting it, run the waste numbers for a month and ask your regulars.² Celine Leblanc's lemon tart is a good example: she nearly cut it, then ranked her menu and found it was her third-highest earner, selling out by ten every morning.

Retire one product at a time and announce it a week ahead.³ About a third of new bakeries close in their first year, so the cost of carrying a weak product is higher than it looks.

## Wrapping Up

You can now rank every product by what it contributes, arrange the case so the best earners sell first, and retire products that do not pay their way.
