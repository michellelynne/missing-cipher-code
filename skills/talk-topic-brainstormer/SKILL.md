# SKILL: Talk Topic Brainstormer

**From:** External Branding (Content Creation)

## When to load this skill

Load this skill when the reader wants to create content, whether a talk, blog post, newsletter,
workshop or short video, and is stuck on what to say.

The blocker heard most often is "I don't have anything to say." That is not true. Even people working
on the same technology have different stories, and the way they tell the details, challenges and
successes will be different. Dry information reads like a textbook; a story is what makes content.

## The brainstorming questions

The skill runs the reader's recent work through these:

1. What is something you have been talking about with your colleagues?
2. Have you recently completed a difficult project?
3. Did you learn a new technology?
4. What is something you are interested in learning?
5. Did you change a process for your team that could help other teams?

## How to run it

1. **Get the raw material.** Recent projects, problems, lessons, and what the reader wants to learn.
2. **Define the audience.** Who exactly they are, what they already know, what they care about, and
   what problem the reader could help them solve.
3. **Generate candidates** from the five questions, each as a one-line pitch with the audience it fits.
4. **Pick the strongest** with the reader: a real story, a clear takeaway, and something the reader is
   excited to talk about.
5. **Draft the abstract,** with a title, two short paragraphs, and what the audience will leave with.
   For a first piece, offer a five-minute lightning talk or short post outline instead.

## Abstract first, build later

A talk can be pitched before it exists. Write an abstract for a project the reader wants to build,
submit it, and build it once it is accepted. A deadline is a forcing function, and nothing is wasted on
a talk nobody booked. If the abstract describes work not done yet, the skill says so plainly so the
reader does not promise results they cannot deliver.

## Prompt the reader can run directly

> I want to give a talk or write a post, but I'm not sure what about. Here is my recent work and what
> I'm interested in: [PASTE]. My audience is [WHO, WHAT THEY KNOW, WHAT THEY CARE ABOUT].
>
> Run my work through these questions: what I've been talking about with colleagues, a difficult
> project I finished, a new technology I learned, something I want to learn, and a process I changed
> that could help other teams. Give me five candidate topics as one-line pitches. Then draft a
> conference abstract for the strongest one: a title, two short paragraphs, and what the audience
> will take away. Keep my real story in it and do not invent results.

## What to tell the reader

- **Rewrite the abstract in your voice.** Reviewers read hundreds; yours should sound like a person.
- **Check what you can share.** Strip confidential details about your employer's systems, customers
  and numbers, and ask if you are unsure.
- **Start small.** Pick one topic and write a five-minute lightning talk, blog post or short video.
- **Timebox it.** A little progress on a regular schedule adds up without burning you out.

## What this skill does not do

It does not write the talk or submit it anywhere. It finds the story you already have and gets it
onto the page.
